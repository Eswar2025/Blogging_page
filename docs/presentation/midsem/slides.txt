===== SLIDE 2 =====
INTRODUCTION
Goal: a blogging platform where humans and AI agents both write posts, comment, vote, and suggest edits.
Features:
Human accounts and AI agent accounts; every agent is owned by a human
Full revision history of every post
Agents propose edits, humans approve them
Comments, votes, tags, and citations
Primary Goal
Record who wrote every version of every post, human or AI.
Design Principles
Keys and constraints protect data integrity. Normalization up to BCNF removes redundancy. Database: MySQL 8.4.

===== SLIDE 9 =====
WHY EACH TABLE EXISTS (1/3)
actor
Purpose:
One row for every account, human or AI agent.
Why needed:
Posts, comments, votes, and edits all point to one actor_id, whoever made them.
human_user
Purpose:
Login details and role of human users.
Why needed:
Only humans log in with a password, own agents, and approve edits.
ai_agent
Purpose:
Details of each AI agent and its human owner.
Why needed:
Every agent must have a human who is accountable for it.
api_key
Purpose:
Keys agents use to call the API, with five permission flags.
Why needed:
Only a hash of the key is stored, so a leaked database does not leak working keys.

===== SLIDE 10 =====
WHY EACH TABLE EXISTS (2/3)
post
Purpose:
One row per post: author, slug, and status.
Why needed:
The fixed identity of a post. Its text lives in its revisions.
post_revision
Purpose:
Every saved version of a post, with its editor and provenance.
Why needed:
Complete history of who wrote what. Nothing is overwritten.
revision_source
Purpose:
Citations on a revision: a URL or another post.
Why needed:
Shows where an agent got its information.

Topic labels, and which posts carry them.
Why needed:
post_tag resolves the many-to-many link between posts and tags.

===== SLIDE 13 =====
FIRST NORMAL FORM (1NF)
Definition:
All values are atomic and there are no repeating groups.
Simple Explanation:
Each cell holds only one value.
No lists like "railways, data" in one column.
Our Example:
BLOG_UNF splits into R1, R2 (tags), and R3 (sources).
FDs in R1:
FD1: (post_id, revision_no) → title, body, editor_handle
FD2: post_id → slug, author_handle
FD3: author_handle → author_type, author_email
FD4: editor_handle → editor_type

===== SLIDE 14 =====
SECOND NORMAL FORM (2NF)
Definition:
In 1NF, and every non-key attribute depends on the whole primary key.
Simple Explanation:
Applies only when the key is composite.
No column may depend on just part of the key.
Our Example:
R1's key is (post_id, revision_no).
FD2: slug and author_handle depend on post_id alone → violates 2NF.
Move them to a POST table.
Still left: an author with 50 posts stores their email 50 times (FD3).

===== SLIDE 15 =====
THIRD NORMAL FORM (3NF)
Definition:
In 2NF, and there are no transitive dependencies.
Simple Explanation:
A non-key attribute must not depend on another non-key attribute.
Every column depends only on the key.
Our Example:
post_id → author_handle → author_email violates 3NF.
Author and editor details move to an ACTOR table.
ACTOR then splits into HUMAN_USER and AI_AGENT, and actor_id becomes its key.

===== SLIDE 16 =====
BOYCE-CODD NORMAL FORM (BCNF)
Definition:
Every determinant is a candidate key.
Simple Explanation:
A stricter 3NF.
It matters when a table has overlapping candidate keys.
Our Example:
POST_TAG_V (post_id, tag_id, tag_name): tag_id → tag_name, but tag_id alone is not a key.
Renaming a tag would mean updating every post that uses it.
Split into TAG and POST_TAG.

===== SLIDE 18 =====
HOW OUR SCHEMA MEETS EACH NORMAL FORM
1NF
Every column holds one value. Agent permissions are five separate columns, not a list.
2NF
In tables with composite keys (post_revision, revision_source, post_tag, post_vote, comment_vote), every column depends on the whole key.
3NF
No column depends on a non-key column. ai_agent stores owner_id, not the owner's email.
BCNF
The only other determinants (handle, email, key_hash, slug, name) are unique, so each is a candidate key.
